# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This guide explains how OctoAcme initiates, plans, delivers, releases, and improves cross-functional projects. The approach is customer-first and iterative, with clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and a focus on psychological safety.

Projects begin with a one-pager that defines the problem, desired outcomes, success measures, stakeholders, timeline, risks, and resource needs. Once the Product Lead and stakeholders align and team capacity is confirmed, the team plans shippable increments in a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, estimates, dependencies, milestones, and a Definition of Done. During execution, the team tracks work on a project board, demonstrates progress at sprint or milestone reviews, and uses metrics to assess delivery and product outcomes.

The Project Manager coordinates schedules, risks, dependencies, and communication; the Product Manager owns product outcomes, priorities, and success measures. Developers build and test the work, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Regular standups and delivery or PM–Product Manager syncs surface progress and blockers; weekly or milestone-based stakeholder updates keep status visible in a shared source of truth. Risks are reviewed regularly, with blockers escalated from the team to the PM, Product Lead, and sponsor as needed.

Quality is checked throughout delivery with acceptance criteria, code review, automated tests and linting, and CI security scanning. Unit and applicable integration tests, critical-flow smoke tests, and manual QA support validation; releases also require passing CI and security checks, release notes, a rollback or mitigation plan, and post-deployment verification. After each sprint, release, milestone, or incident, teams hold retrospectives, assign a few measurable improvement actions to owners, and track follow-up work.
